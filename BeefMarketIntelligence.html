🔗 SUPPLY CHAIN ANALYSIS: How Everything Connects

Cause-and-Effect Relationships

📍 Understanding the Beef Supply Chain Timeline

The beef industry operates on a 2-3 YEAR cycle from breeding decision to finished beef. This creates huge lags between cause (drought, prices) and effect (supply changes). Here's how it works:

Timeframe Event/Decision Market Impact Price Effect
TODAY Drought improves → Ranchers decide to keep heifers instead of selling IMMEDIATE: Fewer heifers in feedlots → Less beef in 6 months Bullish Prices rise due to reduced near-term supply
+9 months Retained heifers are bred Calf crop expands (but calves not born yet) Neutral No immediate impact
+18 months Calves born and weaned Feeder cattle supply starts increasing Bearish Feeder prices start to soften
+24-26 months Calves finish in feedlots → Slaughter Beef production increases, inventory builds Bearish Fed cattle and cutout prices decline

If X Happens, Then Y (Scenario Modeling)

SCENARIO 1: If corn prices rise by $1/bushel...

  • Direct Effect (2-4 weeks): Cost of gain increases by ~$0.18/lb. Feedlot breakevens rise from $178 to $194/cwt.
  • Secondary Effect (6-8 weeks): Feedlots reduce placements by 5-8% to avoid losses. Feeder cattle prices drop $15-20/cwt as demand falls.
  • Tertiary Effect (4-6 months): Reduced placements today = reduced slaughter 4-6 months from now. Fed cattle prices RISE $10-15/cwt due to tighter supplies.
  • Final Effect (12 months): High corn prices encourage cow-calf operators to sell breeding stock (less profitable). Herd expansion delayed, extending bull market.

SCENARIO 2: If Choice cutout rises above $320/cwt...

  • Retail Response (1-2 weeks): Grocery stores reduce beef features/promotions. Ground beef retail hits $6.50+/lb, ribeye hits $18+/lb.
  • Consumer Response (2-4 weeks): Beef demand softens. Consumers trade down (Choice → Select, or beef → chicken). Beef sales volume drops 8-12%.
  • Packer Response (4-6 weeks): Reduced demand = wider Choice-Select spread. Packers shift kill mix toward cows/bulls. Margins compress as cutout falls but live prices stay sticky.
  • Feedlot Response (8-12 weeks): Feedlots accelerate marketings (sell lighter cattle to avoid further losses). This temporarily increases supply, bringing prices back down.

SCENARIO 3: If Mexican cattle imports resume...

  • Immediate (1-2 weeks): Feeder cattle futures drop $8-12/cwt on expectation of increased supply.
  • 30 Days: 75K-100K head/month flow into U.S. feedlots. Feedlot placements spike, cattle-on-feed inventory rises.
  • 90 Days: Mexican feeders begin hitting slaughter. Weekly kill increases by 15-20K head. Choice cutout drops $10-15/cwt on increased availability.
  • 180 Days: Market re-equilibrates at lower price level. Fed cattle prices settle $6-8/cwt below current levels, but increased supply supports packer margins.

Leading vs. Lagging Indicators

Indicator Type Lead Time What It Tells You
Cattle on Feed Placements LEADING 4-6 months Today's placements = slaughter 4-6 months from now. High placements → future supply increase.
Corn Futures (Dec contract) LEADING 3-6 months Predicts future feed costs. Rising corn = feedlot margin pressure = reduced placements = higher cattle prices later.
Feeder Cattle Prices LEADING 4-5 months Input cost for feedlots. High feeder prices = high breakevens = need higher fed cattle prices to be profitable.
CME Deferred Futures LEADING 2-12 months Forward curve shows market expectations. Contango (upward slope) = expected tightening. Backwardation = expected loosening.
Boxed Beef Cutout COINCIDENT Real-time Shows current wholesale demand. Moves with retail/export demand and slaughter volumes.
Weekly Slaughter LAGGING Historical Tells you what already happened. Useful for understanding past supply, but doesn't predict future.
Cold Storage LAGGING Historical Inventory buildup is backward-looking. However, low inventory CAN be a leading indicator of future tight supplies.

Basis Risk Management

What is Basis? Basis = Cash Price - Futures Price. Example: If Texas cash is $189 and June futures are $186, basis is +$3.

Why Basis Matters: You can hedge price risk with futures, but you still have BASIS RISK. If futures and cash both drop $10, you're protected. But if cash drops $15 and futures drop $10, you lose $5 on basis even though you hedged.

Managing Basis Risk:

  • Track historical basis patterns for your region (Texas basis is usually +$2-3 in spring)
  • Use CME feeder cattle index futures (better basis correlation for feeders)
  • Consider basis contracts with packers (locks in basis, you take futures risk)
  • Diversify delivery locations (don't be stuck in weak basis region)
